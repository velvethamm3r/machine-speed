New Google finds half of AI-discovered vulnerabilities give remote code execution, twice the rate of the wider CVE pool

Google Threat Intelligence Group reports that “exactly 50% of all AI-discovered vulnerabilities result in Remote Code Execution (RCE), compared to just 26% across the broader CVE ecosystem,” and that AI-discovered flaws invert the usual severity mix: “58% qualify for Medium Threat Risk (more than double the baseline), while low-risk findings drop to 39%,” against 69% low and 28% medium for the rest. Monthly disclosures doubled from 5,045 in January 2026 to 10,477 in July and 10,740 in August, while exploitation rose from an average of 10.5 a month in 2025 to 18 a month across January–August 2026. The report states two limits on its own figures: “only 0.23% of all disclosed vulnerabilities in 2026 (roughly 1 in 431) were ever observed in active exploitation,” and public data “significantly undercount vulnerabilities discovered by AI” for want of standardised metadata and because of silent first-party and cloud patching.

OpenAI, Google, Meta, Nvidia, xAI and Anthropic signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, a voluntary agreement calling for four layers of oversight: internal controls monitoring model capabilities during training and deployment “with attention to cybersecurity, biosecurity, and chemical threats,” a dedicated internal team to ensure those controls function, independent external audits verifying them, and an independent board committee overseeing the process.
