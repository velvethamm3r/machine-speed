Google DeepMind says AI agents working under human supervision found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA, tested on an already-solved benchmark key

Announced by Google DeepMind's Pushmeet Kohli, per Interesting Engineering, the work credits DeepMind's Dj Dvijotham and Abhradeep Thakurta "in collaboration with researchers from the University of California San Diego and France's Inria". Per that account, the agents showed that once an attacker controls roughly half of a signed block the padding gives virtually no protection, reproducing a known result with 3.5 times less CPU time on an already-solved benchmark key; estimated a theoretical improvement of about 1,600 times on the previous best attack when the attacker controls only a 32-byte digest; and cut the cost of factoring each additional 1024-bit key in a batch by about 30 times. The method was tested in a sandbox on an already-solved RSA-1024 benchmark, and the account says "the findings do not threaten modern encryption", which has largely moved to 2048-bit or larger keys. The AI system used is not named, and DeepMind's own announcement did not open to this run.
