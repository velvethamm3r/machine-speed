Anthropic's safeguards for its proactive agent put the intent check outside the model and keep credentials away from it

Help Net Security reports Anthropic's account of the security and privacy protections for Claude Duet, a proactive agent that monitors connected accounts and works through a cloud-based computer and browser, in a research preview for US Pro and Max subscribers. Sending potentially sensitive messages, making payments and deleting information need user approval, and a separate automated check running outside the model tests whether each action matches the user's intent, which Anthropic describes as a guard against hidden instructions in web pages; accessing someone else's account, trading securities and moving money between bank accounts stay prohibited even with approval, and background routines follow the same rules. A separate classifier is designed to detect sensitive information in outgoing messages, and the checks also cover browser uploads; the agent cannot access passwords, cookies or sign-in tokens, with passwords entered in a form in the Claude app, encrypted and sent to the cloud browser; and the browser runs in a dedicated cloud virtual machine the agent drives by command and cannot read cookies from. Internal red teams tested for data leakage, actions taken without approval and responses to malicious prompts, an external penetration-testing firm reviewed the service with access to its source code, and the service is covered by Anthropic's HackerOne bug bounty. Anthropic says "Approval fatigue is a serious issue, because too many requests teach people to tap through them, sometimes without adequate consideration", warns that password requests claiming to come from Duet through email, text messages or websites other than claude.ai are fraudulent and that legitimate requests appear only during user-initiated sign-ins in the Claude app or on claude.ai, and says the sensitive-data checks may miss content or flag harmless messages during the preview.
